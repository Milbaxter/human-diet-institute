# Experiments that could change what we know

These are proposed study designs. Sample sizes must follow the smallest effect that would change a decision, with allowance for attrition and multiple comparisons.

## 1. Does carnivore have a distinct metabolic advantage?

**Test:** a 12-week randomized feeding trial in adults with insulin resistance: defined carnivore, a ketogenic diet containing plants, and a minimally processed Mediterranean-style diet. Provide food; match protein and support. Adjust energy to maintain weight, verify adherence and ketosis, and prespecify unavoidable nutrient differences.

**Primary outcome:** change in insulin sensitivity measured by a standardized clamp, with blinded assessors. Track ApoB, nutrient status, symptoms and adverse effects separately. The key comparison is carnivore versus plant-containing keto; the third arm contextualizes both.

**Belief changed:** a meaningful advantage would support something beyond weight loss and ketosis. A sufficiently precise null would weaken that claim. Diets remain packages: a positive result would require follow-up experiments to identify which changed foods or nutrients caused it.

**Requires:** a feeding center, clinical partner and substantial funding. This is the flagship study.

## 2. Do responders need to exclude all plants—or specific foods?

**Test:** a coordinated series of randomized, repeated food reintroductions among adults reporting stable digestive improvement on carnivore. Use one shared protocol, a prespecified set of foods, matched comparison preparations, blinded challenges where feasible, and adequate recovery periods. Record allocation guesses to assess blinding. Clinicians screen participants; exclude allergy challenges.

**Primary outcome:** within-person change in a validated digestive-symptom score. Define a reproducible response before enrollment; model repeated observations and estimate how responses vary across people. Have an independent site repeat the same contrasts.

**Belief changed:** reliably tolerated foods would challenge the necessity of total plant exclusion for those responders. Repeatable food-specific reactions could support a more precise diet. This selected sample cannot establish how often carnivore helps the general population.

**Start here:** recruit a gastroenterology/dietetics collaborator and preregister one challenge with a blinded comparator where practical. Pilot logistics, then run a properly sized study and independent replication. Menu audits support preparation; they are not the institute's scientific destination.

## 3. Do benefits survive ordinary life, and what are the costs?

**Test:** if short-term evidence and safety justify it, a multicenter, 12-month randomized comparison of carnivore and the strongest alternative from study 1, in a prespecified population with prediabetes. Match support, retain participants who discontinue, and track medication changes.

**Primary outcome:** HbA1c at 12 months under intention-to-treat analysis. Prespecify separate safety outcomes, including ApoB and nutrient deficiencies, plus adherence, quality of life and cost. Continue consented follow-up for clinical events.

**Belief changed:** durable benefit, acceptable burden and the safety profile would inform whether larger trials are justified. Biomarkers cannot settle lifetime cardiovascular or cancer risk; those claims require much larger, longer studies. Observational follow-up remains vulnerable to confounding.

## Build on existing work

- [Carnivore survey, 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34934897/): a source of hypotheses; self-selection prevents causal inference.
- [Keto-Med trial, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35641199/): metabolic and lipid outcomes can move differently; ketogenic is not synonymous with carnivore.
- [Registered carnivore/ketogenic trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07524244): already addresses inflammatory bowel disease and rheumatoid arthritis. Our proposed contribution emphasizes controlled feeding and reproducible reintroduction. Check publications and registries again before committing resources.

All human studies require appropriate ethics review, consent, clinical oversight and prespecified stopping rules. Publish negative results with the same care as positive ones.

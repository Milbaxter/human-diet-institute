# First experiments

These are proposals, not finalized clinical protocols. Run one at a time.

## 1. Can specified carnivore menus meet nutrient targets?

**Hypothesis:** a varied animal-food menu can meet selected adult nutrient reference intakes without supplements.

Freeze three seven-day menus before calculating: meat-only; animal foods including fish, eggs and dairy; and an omnivorous comparator. Match energy and protein within declared tolerances. State food weights, preparation, target adult age/sex, reference-intake source and permitted ingredients.

Use a fixed [USDA FoodData Central release](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/). Primary outcome: number of prespecified nutrients below their reference intake in the seven-day average. Report every nutrient assessed, upper-limit exceedances and missing values; missing is not zero. Check how substitutions change the result.

**Deliverable:** menus, food IDs, source snapshot, calculation script and table that another person independently recreates. This tests those menus against reference values, not absorption, deficiency or health outcomes.

## 2. Does carnivore outperform a minimally processed mixed diet for one person?

**Hypothesis:** carnivore reduces a prespecified recurring digestive symptom more than an energy- and protein-matched mixed diet.

After clinician review, design repeated periods in randomized order. Fix menus, adaptation time, washout, total duration and analysis before starting; allow enough time for the chosen outcome to stabilize. Track the same daily symptom score, adherence, weight, sleep and exercise. Compare period averages, accounting for order and carryover; daily observations are not independent participants.

Primary outcome: mean symptom severity, with a meaningful improvement threshold chosen beforehand. Track tolerability and clinician-selected safety measures, including blood lipids/ApoB where appropriate. Agree on numeric safety limits and symptom-based stopping rules before starting; do not change medication independently.

**What it resolves:** whether the complete diet helps this person beyond a minimally processed comparator. Differences in carbohydrate, fiber and fat remain possible explanations. Short-term symptom improvement cannot establish long-term safety.

## 3. Is any benefit explained by a particular food exclusion?

For someone already benefiting from an elimination diet, a clinician/dietitian can plan repeated, randomized reintroductions of one suspected food against a tolerated comparison. Prespecify dose, recovery interval and the same symptom outcome. Do not challenge known or suspected food allergies at home.

Reproducible symptoms from one food support a targeted exclusion; tolerated reintroductions weaken the claim that all plants must be excluded. Unblinded expectations remain a limitation.

## Evidence to challenge us

- [Lennerz et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34934897/): carnivore survey with favorable self-reports; self-selection and no control group prevent causal conclusions.
- [Hall et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31105044/): randomized feeding study supports food processing as a competing explanation for changes in intake and weight.
- [Gardner et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35641199/): ketogenic versus Mediterranean trial found tradeoffs, including higher LDL on ketogenic. Neither diet was carnivore.

Human experiments need an individual safety plan. Before recruiting others, obtain appropriate ethics review and informed consent.

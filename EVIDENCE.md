# What we know—and the gap to test

Selected starting evidence, checked 19 September 2026. This is not a systematic review. Direct evidence about carnivore and evidence about possible explanations are kept distinct.

| Evidence | What it tells us | What remains unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Carnivore survey — Lennerz et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34934897/) | 2,029 respondents reported substantial benefits; a subset reporting lipids also reported elevated LDL. | Self-selection, self-report and no control group prevent conclusions about causation or long-term safety. |
| [Controlled feeding — Hall et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31105044/) | In 20 adults, an ultra-processed diet increased energy intake and weight relative to an unprocessed diet. | Removing processed food is a competing explanation for some improvements attributed to carnivore; this was not a carnivore trial. |
| [Keto-Med — Gardner et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35641199/) | The primary analysis found no significant HbA1c difference between diets; ketogenic eating produced lower triglycerides but higher LDL. | Both diets included plants. This cannot establish the added value of excluding all plants, and different health outcomes can move in opposite directions. |
| [Blinded food challenge — Skodje et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29102613/) | In people with self-reported gluten sensitivity, fructans produced more symptoms than gluten; gluten did not differ from placebo. | A precedent for separating suspected ingredients, not evidence about carnivore responders or all plant foods. |

**Related research:** [NCT07524244](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07524244) registers ketogenic/carnivore interventions for inflammatory bowel disease and rheumatoid arthritis. Registration is not a result. Check its updates and other trials before claiming novelty or finalizing our study.

## Our working gap

These sources do not establish whether people reporting digestive improvement on carnivore need total plant exclusion to maintain it. Repeated, controlled reintroductions could test the necessity of excluding particular foods in that selected population.

**Next evidence task:** search published studies and trial registries specifically for this comparison, including negative findings. Record the search date, terms, eligibility rules and missing data. If the question is already answered, reproduce or sharpen that result before proposing another study.

For each future claim, preserve its source, population, comparator, uncertainty and strongest alternative explanation. A citation must support the claim, not merely exist.
